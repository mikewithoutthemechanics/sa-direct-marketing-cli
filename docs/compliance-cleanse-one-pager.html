Three things that will cost you

The one everyone gets wrong

1. B2B is covered

The 2026 Amendment Regulations apply to promotional activity aimed at companies, not just consumers. POPIA section 69 applies regardless of how the CPA classifies the recipient. If you email or WhatsApp a business, you are in scope.

Consent is not enough

2. A block overrides your opt-in

Someone who registers a pre-emptive block must not be marketed to again — even if they opted in with you before, and even if they never told you.

The silent one

3. Blocks can be scoped to your whole industry

The registry supports blocks by company, industry, channel, purpose or time period. A plumber can block plumbers in a region. You will not receive a notification, an email, or a list of who to drop. You just keep sending.

Fair questions

"We are small. Would anyone really come after us?"
The penalty is a flat fine of up to R1m or 10% of turnover, not a percentage of your spend. A single campaign to a blocked consumer is enough. Size is not a defence.
"We do WhatsApp, not marketing."
Promotional messages to a list are direct marketing, including follow-ups and quote requests to people who did not ask. Most businesses pass this test.
"Isn't this just POPIA again?"
Different problem, same shape. The registry is new, it is a government portal, and it has no API — so the export and cross-match is manual. That manual step is exactly where businesses fall over.
"Is this legal advice?"
No. This is a managed compliance service, not a law firm. For your specific obligations, speak to an attorney. We will tell you what the regulations require and prove what we did — we will not tell you what you should conclude.
